# Commission credit and direct-pay model: narrow risk note (8 November 2026)

**This is a risk note to help the owner brief a Zimbabwean lawyer and Paynow. It is not legal advice and it does not say ConZ is exempt or compliant.**

**Limits of this research.** The reviewer could not open the primary text of any key instrument: ZimLII returned 403, PolicyVault 503, regalert 429, and the Gazette PDF of the Consumer Protection Act came back unreadable. Everything below comes from search snippets and secondary summaries. Treat every row as unverified until a lawyer reads the operative text. About 13 tool calls were used.

## What was reviewed

The ConZ V1 model: two participants (clients and independent drivers); the client pays the driver directly (cash, EcoCash, bank transfer; any deposit is also paid to the driver); ConZ earns 7% of the agreed transport fare from a prepaid commission credit the driver tops up through Paynow; the credit is intended to be usable only for ConZ commission, not withdrawable. ConZ is operated by Navysky Innovations (Private) Limited.

## Stated by source, our reading, unknown

- **Stated by source (secondary):** NPS Act s18 is headed "Prohibition against payment intermediation". RBZ guidelines define e-money as stored value that is a claim on an issuer. SI 17/2025 sets the fees above. POTRAZ licensing under SI 155 exists.
- **Reading (inference):** the "claim on the issuer" definition and the s18 heading suggest the risk centres on holding value for others and moving it between parties. ConZ's design avoids moving the client's fare. A top-up that ConZ keeps as its own prepaid fee is the grey area.
- **Unknown:** the operative text of s18 and the definitions; whether a closed-loop fee credit is caught; penalties; whether Paynow's licence covers this use.

## Risk note

**Verified.** Zimbabwe regulates payments through the NPS Act's recognition regime, RBZ guidelines on retail instruments and e-money, and the SI 80/2020 money-transmission rules. S18 is headed "Prohibition against payment intermediation". Consumer law is now the Consumer Protection Act 2019, and POTRAZ has been licensing data controllers since SI 155 of 2024. No source found says a prepaid balance spendable only on the operator's own fees is regulated, or that it is exempt.

**Uncertain.** The s18 text and definitions were never read. It is unknown whether "intermediation" covers merely enabling a direct client-to-driver payment, or whether a commission credit counts as e-money or stored value; how the RBZ treats a restricted, non-withdrawable closed-loop balance; whether ConZ needs a POTRAZ licence; how the Consumer Protection Act applies to a marketplace; and the tax treatment.

**Realistic worst cases (not predictions).**
1. The RBZ or Paynow treats the credit as unlicensed stored value or intermediation: ConZ would have to stop top-ups, restructure to charge the commission after each job, or partner with a licensed provider.
2. Paynow suspends the merchant account if its terms bar this use.
3. POTRAZ enforcement for having no controller licence (2026 press reports describe intensifying enforcement).
4. An unfair-terms challenge to non-refundable credit or broad disclaimers.
5. A tax assessment on the commission or on top-up levies.

Wording matters: screens that say "wallet", "balance" or "we hold" invite the stored-value reading.

## Questions for a Zimbabwean lawyer

1. Quote NPS Act s18 and the definition of "payment intermediation". Does a platform that only connects a client and a driver, with no funds touching ConZ, fall within it?
2. Is a prepaid credit usable only for ConZ's own commission, with no cash-out, an "instrument", "e-money" or "stored value" under the RBZ guidelines? Is any RBZ notification or no-objection needed?
3. Does refunding an unused credit to the driver change that answer?
4. Does the Consumer Protection Act (s41, s42) apply to ConZ's terms with drivers and to the client-driver agreement? Can ConZ disclaim responsibility for the driver's service?
5. Which Cyber and Data Protection Act provisions give a PIN confirmation, a chat or offer record and GPS evidence legal weight? Must the electronic-transactions law be consulted?
6. Must Navysky hold a POTRAZ data controller licence and appoint a data protection officer? Who is the processor for Supabase and Vercel, which sit outside Zimbabwe? Is a cross-border transfer notice needed?
7. Is the commission VAT-able, does Navysky need to register, and who bears IMTT on top-ups?

## Questions for Paynow

8. Does your merchant agreement allow us to collect prepaid top-ups for the operator's own fees? In what name do the funds settle?
9. What must we tell drivers about refund, dispute and chargeback rules for top-ups?
10. Do you consider this use as needing RBZ approval beyond your own licence?
11. Who pays IMTT and fees on a top-up, and how is it shown on the receipt?
12. Can you supply a written statement of your licensed scope that we can show a lawyer?

## Wording to avoid on screens and in the terms

| Avoid | Safer plain wording |
|---|---|
| wallet, "your balance" | "Commission credit" or "prepaid commission" |
| escrow, "we hold your money" | "We reserve commission from your credit"; "Your fare is paid directly to the driver" |
| deposit to ConZ | "Top up commission credit" |
| withdraw, cash out | Do not offer it. State "Credit can only be used for ConZ commission fees." |
| we pay drivers, payout | "Client pays driver directly" |
| "safe", "guaranteed payment" | "ConZ does not hold or guarantee fare payments" |
| interest, savings, earnings on credit | Never say these |
| "ConZ pays or refunds the fare" | "Agree refunds with the driver." State ConZ's own refund policy for credit separately. |
| "Funds are protected or insured" | Avoid unless true |

## Recent changes found (2025 to 2026), all secondary

- **SI 17 of 2025** (28 Feb 2025) amends SI 80 of 2020 with the fees above.

- **RBZ QR-code guidelines, March 2026** are listed by regalert (not read).

## What this means for the build

Nothing in this note changes what is built; it governs wording and what must be put to a lawyer before the commission-credit model is described to drivers as "safe" or "compliant". Screens must use the safer wording above. The credit stays restricted to ConZ commission, with no cash-out feature added.
