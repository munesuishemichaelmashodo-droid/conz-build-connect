# Keep ConZ deliveries moving without signal

**Today a ConZ driver with no data at the drop-off cannot finish the job, and the fix is mostly "save now, send later", not a new network.** The code audit shows that every driver step needs data and nothing is queued. The delivery photo and the delivery PIN are both hard server requirements, so a driver who reaches the gate in a dead zone can take the photo but cannot record it, and the job cannot complete. The best-documented practice, from field-data and driver apps, is to capture proof on the phone, keep it visibly pending, and confirm only when the server receives it. No product I found checks a delivery code offline. The recommendation is the same split: a driver may capture and queue evidence offline, the server alone verifies the PIN and decides, and nothing moves money without a server check. The cheapest, safest work is web-only: a "Ready offline" job card with the drop-off details stored on the phone, a durable outbox for photos, honest "no signal" messages, and a few server changes so late evidence keeps its true capture time. Anything that needs a new Android release (a custom start-up screen, background GPS) or an SMS provider (server-sent texts, two-way SMS) should come after that. Coverage is the biggest unknown: 2G reaches about 94% of Zimbabweans but 4G only about half to 59%, and no source says anything about quarry, sand-river or farm roads. Nothing here was tested on a real phone.

## The decisions that matter

Six decisions shape everything else; the rest of the report explains them.

1. **Offline means "captured", never "completed".** The driver's screen may say "Delivery saved on this phone". The job stays "delivered, waiting for confirmation" until the server has the photo and has checked the PIN. Recommended: yes.
2. **The PIN is checked only by the server, never on the driver's phone.** A 6-digit PIN has about 20 bits, so a phone that can verify it can be made to reveal it ([RFC 4226](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4226) sets the minimum at 6 digits and says attempt limits belong on a trusted verifier). Recommended: yes, no exceptions.
3. **Does a PIN that is verified late (hours after the drop) settle the job automatically?** Today a correct PIN triggers settlement at once. The research notes disagree on whether to queue the PIN at all, and the owner has to settle it (see the first open question). Recommended for Phase 1: queue the photos, but have the driver enter the PIN once signal returns. Queue the PIN itself in Phase 2, with the late-evidence tiers in place.
4. **Late evidence is shown honestly, never hidden and never punished automatically.** The server's receive time is the trusted clock. The phone's time is a claim, shown with how it was estimated.
5. **Software first, Android release second, providers third.** Phases 0 to 2 need no Play Store update. A new Android build and Play declarations come in Phase 3. SMS and WhatsApp need a provider and written quotes, and USSD is not worth pursuing yet.
6. **A human fallback stays in the design.** Every peer app ends in a phone call, and Zimbabwean drivers will use voice and SMS where data fails. A staffed dispatcher line, with admin review of stuck jobs, is part of the product.

## What ConZ does today without signal: every driver step stops

The audit read the code at `b8089d4`, the location-sharing and ETA fix, which has since been merged to `main` as #78 (`5cca7d3`), so it describes what is live. Nothing was run on a device. Library behaviour was read from `node_modules`. The short version is that ConZ is strictly online-first, and the failures are silent: raw error text in toasts, empty results that look like real answers, and an endless "Loading…".

| Moment | What happens with no data | What is lost |
|---|---|---|
| Open the Android app | The shell only points a WebView at conz.co.zw. Nothing is bundled and no custom error page is set. The only service worker handles push, not caching. The driver sees Android's default error page, and a weak signal can hang for 30 seconds or more ([Capacitor discussion #8627](https://github.com/ionic-team/capacitor/discussions/8627)). | The whole app |
| Network drops mid-screen | No screen shows connectivity. Several lookups turn a failure into an empty "success": a failed phone lookup disables the Call button ("No phone number on their profile yet"), a failed roles read can show "Only customer accounts can book deliveries". The job page ends on "Loading…" with no retry. | Anything not already in memory |
| Reopen a job offline | Nothing from the job is stored on the phone. The signed-in guard calls the network and redirects to sign-in on any error. | The job card, PIN screen, drop-off pin |
| Accept an offer, bid, withdraw | Single online calls. Dispatch offers last 60 seconds or 5 minutes, bids 30 minutes. A failed accept closes the modal. | The offer, silently |
| Arrive at pickup, Start trip | One online call each. The arrival time recorded is the server's time when the call lands, so a late tap is a late time. A failed Start trip leaves a manual "Start trip" card. | The true arrival moment |
| Pickup and delivery photo | The GPS fix is requested only after the camera returns (up to about 16 seconds). The raw photo is uploaded without compression (the bucket allows 10 MiB). Each retry uses a new random file name. The only retry is three quick attempts (about 1.8 seconds in total) to record the photo after it has uploaded. | The photo, if the app is killed |
| Delivery PIN | The code is 6 digits, held on the server, readable only by the customer. Five wrong tries lock for 15 minutes, ten for 24 hours, and it lasts 7 days. The server evaluates it only after a recorded delivery photo exists. | Nothing to queue today |
| Live GPS | Opt-in per delivery, foreground only, dropped (never queued) when data is down. Before #78 the screen kept saying "On" while pushes failed; since #78 (merged) it warns after 90 seconds without an update and resumes after a reload. Locking the screen or opening Google Maps pauses sharing. | Position history for the gap |
| Chat | Messages show "Not sent, tap to retry", but the retry list lives in memory only and messages carry no unique id. | Unsent messages on a reload |
| Customer watching | A driver position older than 10 minutes stops being "Live" and the ETA disappears, but the message always blames the truck ("The truck may have moved since"). It cannot tell the customer's own dropped connection from a quiet driver. The shared link needs JavaScript to load and shows nothing without data. | Trust: the customer cannot tell whose fault it is |
| Customer's PIN | Fetched over the network and kept in memory only. If the fetch failed, the card shows "······". | The ability to show the code at the gate |
| Maps | CARTO raster tiles that need a key. Without one they are watermarked "API KEY REQUIRED". Routes come from the public OSRM demo server. | Any map, any route |
| SMS | Only a one-time-code hook for sign-in. It cannot send job updates. | |

Three findings sit underneath that table. First, evidence time and place are stamped at upload, not capture: `uploaded_at` and the photo-taken times are the database clock when the record is written, and the photo's coordinates are the phone's fix when the upload starts. A photo synced an hour late would be time-shifted and place-shifted. That would distort the pickup check (flag over 2 km), the "metres from the delivery address" line in the admin dispute panel, the 72-hour escrow auto-release clock and the customer's "Loaded" and "Delivered" times. Second, the 24-hour sweep auto-cancels accepted jobs with no pickup photo, so a driver whose pickup photo is waiting in a queue could be cancelled while still honest. Third, the repo's own notes record seven direct-pay jobs stuck 19 to 88 days with a pickup photo and no delivery photo (cause not stated, not verified here). That pattern is what a delivery-stage connectivity problem would leave, so it is worth checking before building anything.

## How African and comparable apps solve this: capture offline, confirm online

The strongest peer evidence is not from ride-hailing, which assumes connectivity. It comes from field-data and driver apps, and its message is consistent.

**Capture offline, confirm online.** Upper lets drivers view the route they already hold, complete or skip stops and capture proof offline, while live location, dispatch updates and messaging pause ([Upper](https://support.upperinc.com/docs/crew/mobile-driver-app/features-available-in-offline-mode)). Dispatch opens the proof-of-delivery screen offline, saves it as a draft and tells the driver the remaining steps once online ([Dispatch](https://intercom.help/dispatch-driver/en/articles/8660379-how-do-i-complete-a-delivery-in-an-area-with-no-cell-phone-service)). Onfleet is the cautious end: starting or completing tasks is blocked offline by default, and an optional "Offline Assist" window is 2 minutes by default, up to 8 hours on request ([Onfleet](https://intercom.help/onfleet-4f83d83093ac/en/articles/15254706)). Geotab's telematics devices store records with their time and send them when coverage returns, and the app labels them temporary until then ([Geotab](https://support.geotab.com/geotab-drive/doc/offline-mode)). No source documents any production system verifying a delivery code offline. DoorDash's PIN has a "cannot collect PIN" path that falls back to a photo and a description ([DoorDash](https://help.doordash.com/en-us/dashers/article/doordash-pin-deliveries-or-tasks)).

**The server's receive time is the trusted clock.** CommCare says server-set times "can usually be trusted" while phone times can be wrong, and its example of 25 forms submitted at month end shows why submission dates mislead ([Dimagi](https://dimagi.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/commcarepublic/pages/2143963531/Understanding+Times+and+Dates)). Android itself warns the wall clock "can be set by the user or the phone network" ([Android SystemClock](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/os/SystemClock)), and only GPS-provider fixes carry satellite time ([Android Location](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/location/Location)).

**Small, cached and human-assisted.** Uber Lite capped each screen to one small request and cached destinations for poor connections ([Uber Engineering](https://www.uber.com/blog/engineering-uber-lite/)). Ola let a rider whose booking failed send a pre-filled SMS from their own phone ([Voice&Data](https://www.voicendata.com/ola-launches-offline-booking-on-its-app)). Kenya's Little offered USSD booking ([Safaricom](https://safaricom.co.ke/media-center-landing/press-releases/taxi-hailing-app-little-now-available-via-ussd)), and Hello Tractor uses human booking agents plus an app with offline modules ([IITA](https://propas.iita.org/en/solutions/contract-mechanization-apps-eg-hello-tractor/75/details/)). inDrive and Bolt in South Africa removed the data cost altogether by zero-rating with an operator ([BusinessLive](https://www.businesslive.co.za/bd/companies/telecoms-and-technology/2023-01-25-indrive-goes-data-free-to-stand-out-in-sa-market/)). Nothing suggests such a deal exists for any Zimbabwean app. For hard-to-find drop-offs, Uber Eats shows customer notes and gate codes, lets the courier call or message, sends a "didn't answer" notification, and then lets the courier end the delivery ([Uber Eats](https://www.uber.com/in/en/deliver/basics/tips-for-success/delivering-orders/)). Jumia couriers in Abidjan stay on the phone for landmarks ("No one can rely on GPS") ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/africa/a-delivery-order-with-no-address-1.66850236)).

**Fleet rules for dead zones are human rules.** Canadian journey-management guidance uses a written trip plan, a named check-in contact, check-ins about every 2 hours (tighter at higher risk), a call before leaving and after regaining coverage, and a fixed escalation: driver, then the destination, then a supervisor ([Road Safety at Work](https://roadsafetyatwork.ca/journey-management/)). For ConZ the customer is the destination contact and a human dispatcher decides. No southern African fleet rulebook was found.

**Fallback channels fit Zimbabwe unevenly.** The reach argument is real: 2G covers about 94% of the population against 4G at 58.8% ([Agence Ecofin](https://www.osiris.sn/zimbabwe-les-consommateurs-face-a-la-fin-de-la-2g-et-de-la-3g.html)), and Econet's 2G is "widely used in rural areas" though 3G is due to go by end-2027 ([NewsDay](https://www.newsday.co.zw/article/econet-says-to-phase-out-3g-by-end-of-2027)). But server-sent SMS is priced at about **US$0.10 to 0.33 per message** (Twilio US$0.3212, [Twilio](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/sms/pricing/zw); Plivo US$0.1015 to 0.3289, [Plivo](https://www.plivo.com/sms/pricing/zw)). Sender-ID rules conflict across providers, Twilio says registration is required but "not supported", and it offers no two-way SMS ([Twilio guidelines](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/guidelines/zw/sms)). Telecel allows only one-time-code style messages ([D7 Networks](https://d7networks.com/sms/zimbabwe/sms-regulations/)). WhatsApp utility messages cost about US$0.004 in third-party tables but still need data, and calls no longer work on cheap WhatsApp bundles ([Techzim](https://www.techzim.co.zw/2025/10/netone-whatsapp-bundles-no-longer-support-whatsapp-calls)). USSD needs a separate agreement per operator, possibly POTRAZ approval, has no published 2026 price ([IBZim](https://www.ibzim.com/telecom/guides/how-to-register-econet-or-netone-business-shortcode)), is not listed for Zimbabwe by Africa's Talking ([Africa's Talking](https://help.africastalking.com/en/articles/2727792-which-countries-are-africa-s-talking-products-in)), and went down for days in Econet's March 2024 data-centre fault ([Techzim](https://www.techzim.co.zw/2024/03/heres-whats-causing-technical-challenges-on-econets-network/)). Voice is native: the driver can already call, and Econet's call-me-back and NetOne's Reverse Calling exist ([ZimSphere](https://www.zimsphere.co.zw/2026/01/netone-introduces-reverse-calling.html)).

Three technical limits shape the build. Android WebView has no Background Sync ([MDN compat data](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/main/api/SyncManager.json)), so a queue must flush while the app is open. The browser's geolocation spec drops position updates while the page is hidden ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/geolocation/)), so keeping tracking alive with the screen off needs a native foreground service, which in turn needs a Play Console declaration ([Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13392821)). Capacitor says the `server.url` mode ConZ uses is "not intended for use in production" ([Capacitor](https://capacitorjs.com/docs/config)), so a cold start with no network is a known weak point, not a bug to patch quickly.

## Recommended build order

The order below favours the least effort and risk first, and it keeps native releases and providers apart from web work.

**Phase 0: days, web only, no database change.** Start measuring: log each driver session's WebView version, connection type, failed request counts and durations, upload success and time, GPS gaps and mid-trip relaunches. This decides how much of Phases 2 and 3 is needed. Also fix D4 (the offline reload loop and silent empty results), ship D3's banner with plain wording, confirm that the production build has a valid CARTO key (unkeyed tiles are watermarked; CARTO's commercial terms also changed on 1 December 2026 ([CARTO](https://carto.com/legal/basemap-terms/))), and read the seven stuck direct-pay jobs to see whether connectivity caused them.

**Phase 1: 3 to 5 weeks, web plus two small migrations, no Android release.** This phase gives the driver a way to finish and the customer a way to see what happened.
- *Server contract (migration):* make `record_job_evidence` safe to repeat, let it accept a bounded client capture time and location with a "method" label, store the server receive time separately, and let arrival calls take a bounded client time. Make sure the 24-hour no-show sweep and the 72-hour auto-release clock use server receipt and recent heartbeats, not sync-time stamps.
- *Phone side:* D1 (photo pipeline and outbox), D2 and C3's minimal fields (job card and drop-off card), D9 (checklist), D10 (call, problem, unable-to-locate), C1 (PIN cache), C2 (honest status), C4 and C5.
- *Reconnect:* refetch the active job, offers and notifications on app resume, reconnect and visibility change, because Supabase says Realtime "doesn't guarantee every message gets delivered" ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/troubleshooting/realtime-postgres-changes-troubleshooting.md)).
- *Rollout:* gate each piece behind the existing `launch_switches` pattern and test on the cheapest real phones, including a Tecno, Infinix or itel handset (Transsion held 51% of African shipments in Q2 2025, [Canalys](https://canalys.com/newsroom/africa-smartphone-market-q2-2025), and those phones can halt apps that are swiped away, [dontkillmyapp](https://dontkillmyapp.com/tecno)).

**Phase 2: 4 to 8 weeks, web only.** D6 (route replay, navigation buttons and a self-hosted or licensed router), D5's Plus Code, D8 (GPS buffer and batch upload), D7 (deferred PIN, after the payments decision), admin dispute display of capture time versus receipt time with the tiers below, C6 and C7. Add D11 (offline map packs) for the driver trip view only if Phase 0 shows many drivers in dead zones.

**Phase 3: needs a new Android build and Play declarations.** Cheap first: a native start-up overlay or bundled error page (small to medium), and a Capacitor network plugin for better connectivity events. Then, only if telemetry shows large GPS gaps or disputes, the foreground-service GPS plugin (D12). Play requires an Android 14+ foreground-service declaration with a video and a persistent notification, and applies stricter rules to background location ([Google Play location policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9799150)). Paid plugins with offline buffering cost roughly US$399 one-off or US$990 a year according to a competitor's comparison, so verify with each vendor ([Capawesome](https://capawesome.io/blog/alternative-to-transistorsoft-background-geolocation/)). Budget extra weeks for Play review rounds (Google states no timeline). A service worker (D13) is also a Phase 3 candidate on the web side, because it is the first service worker the shell would ever run and needs a kill switch. A fully bundled app with live updates is a strategic, XL decision and not recommended as a quick offline fix.

**Phase 4: needs a provider or operator arrangement.** Get written quotes from local aggregators with direct operator connections (Celcom Africa and others claim them; none published prices). Ask for delivery receipts, two-way SMS, a registered alphanumeric sender ID, and what Telecel will deliver. Only then build C8 and the optional inbound-SMS idea for sending an evidence receipt when there is voice and SMS signal but no data (an operator timestamp is an independent clock). Treat WhatsApp (C9) the same way. Do not pursue USSD until volume justifies an operator relationship. A dispatcher phone line is a staffing decision, not software. Any stored SMS or call records add to ConZ's personal data, which plausibly falls under Zimbabwe's data-controller licensing and 24-hour breach reporting ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperafrica.com/zimbabwe/insights/2024/A-Quick-Start-Guide-to-Zimbabwes-Data-Protection-Regulations)); confirm this with Zimbabwean counsel.

## Operating rules to adopt

The thresholds are proposed defaults to tune from pilot data. They are product choices, anchored to the existing 72-hour auto-release and the 24-hour upload-session limit, not documented standards.

| Rule area | Rule |
|---|---|
| **May do offline** | View the saved job card and drop-off card. Mark arrival at pickup (a claim, queued). Capture the loading photo and "where did you load". Start trip (shown as "waiting to send"). Mark arrival at drop-off. Take the delivery photos. Enter the PIN once (Phase 2 only). Write chat messages. File a problem report with a photo. Record the GPS trail. |
| **Must do online** | Accept or decline an offer, place or change a bid. Cancel a job (offer "Report a problem" instead). Wallet, withdrawal and anything money-related. Profile and sign-in changes. Completion itself. The server alone checks the PIN and moves the job to completed. |
| **Never queued** | Anything that settles, releases or moves money. Offer acceptance (offers expire). Cancellation. |
| **How long queued actions may wait** | Unsent evidence stays on the phone until the server confirms it and is never auto-deleted. Server treatment is by receipt time: up to about 15 minutes after capture is "live"; up to 24 hours with corroboration (a gap in the driver's pings, GPS fixes present) is normal; 24 to 72 hours, or a gap with no corroboration, goes to admin review before any payment-affecting step; over 72 hours, or arriving after a dispute opened or the job auto-closed, is kept and shown but labelled "late" and decided by an admin only. A driver with evidence over 24 hours old waiting cannot accept a new job. Chat held longer than about 2 hours is not sent without asking the driver, and recipients see "sent late" past a few minutes. Retry starts at about 30 seconds, doubles to a cap of about 15 minutes with jitter, resets on a real network success, and moves to "needs help" (never deleted) after 72 hours. |
| **How late evidence is shown** | Admin: one timeline row per event with capture estimate and the method used (phone timer anchored to receipt, satellite time, or phone clock only), phone clock reading, receipt time, photo-received time, delay, signal gaps, and flags. Customer: plain language and no blame, with the late note shown only above 15 to 30 minutes ("Photo taken 2:05 pm. It reached ConZ at 4:41 pm because the driver had no signal."). Driver: "Taken 2:05 pm. Sent 4:41 pm. Received by ConZ", plus a neutral "ConZ will check this one" with a way to add a note. |
| **How late evidence is treated in disputes** | Device times are claims, server times are facts. Evidence is always kept and shown; its weight varies, not its admissibility. A late arrival is not a violation, but an unexplained late arrival repeated across jobs is a signal. Evidence whose first receipt arrives after a dispute opens is labelled "late, after dispute". Rough order of weight: server-checked PIN, then the customer's in-app confirmation, then a photo with a GPS fix and an early receipt, then a photo alone, then a driver's statement (vendor guidance, treat as a starting point). Ask the customer for their own evidence too. |
| **No automatic punishment** | Never penalise a driver on one flag, clock skew or lateness alone. Require two independent signals (for example mock location plus a duplicate photo, or repeated unexplained late receipts), and let the driver respond. A survey-fraud model that was 96% accurate still wrongly flagged about 4% of valid cases, so flags are triage ([FedCASIC 2023](https://census.gov/fedcasic/fc2023/pdf/7a-Herdegen.pdf)). Offer separate reasons: no signal, no data bundle, phone died. |
| **How the delivery PIN works with no network** | The PIN is never on the driver's phone and is never checked there. Offline, the screen says: "No signal. Your photos are saved. The delivery is confirmed only after the code is checked." Phase 1: the driver enters the code once signal returns. Phase 2: the driver may type it twice offline; the phone stores it encrypted, shows "PIN saved, not checked yet", and sends it after the photo receipt. The customer sees "Driver says delivered" when it arrives and "PIN used at HH:MM" after the server checks it. Wrong entries count towards the existing lockout (5 wrong tries lock for 15 minutes, 10 for 24 hours), the PIN is single-use and job-bound, and an offline entry counts as one attempt. If the customer cannot give the code, the driver records a reason and a photo; the job becomes "delivered, unverified" and needs the customer's confirmation or admin review, never auto-completion. |
| **How the customer is told what is happening** | Plain words, with the customer's own connection separated from the driver's: "Your connection is down" versus "No update from [driver] since 2:05 pm." Show last known place and time, not an alarm. Use "no signal" or "no data bundle" rather than "offline". Use a calm banner, not red. Escalation order when a driver is silent: driver, then customer as destination contact, then a human dispatcher. Customer screens show no technical flags. |
| **Evidence quality** | In-app camera only for delivery proof (the gallery option today stamps any old image with a fresh GPS fix labelled "captured"). Capture time and location at capture, not upload. Keep a signed record of each item (hash, capture clock readings, GPS fix, mock-location flag, app version) in a sidecar, because photo metadata is editable and stripped by compression. At least two context photos (the load, the drop spot). Duplicate and near-duplicate photo checks across the platform after sync. Record mock-location on every fix, and treat true as a strong flag but false as no proof. |
| **Driver duties** | Open the trip map with location on before entering a dead zone (satellite data used by GPS stays valid for about four hours, so a warm start is faster). Declare "no signal" at the last good spot or call dispatch (fleet check-in practice). Keep "automatic date and time" on. Do not sign out, clear data or uninstall with items waiting. |
| **Payments dependency (note only)** | Any transition that releases or settles money must key off server-verified events and server timestamps, never a queued claim. The 72-hour auto-release must be anchored to server receipt of evidence. The 24-hour no-show sweep must account for a queued pickup photo and recent driver heartbeats. Direct-pay completion must require the same evidence and PIN before it can follow a queued delivery. These belong to the payments owner and are not designed here. |

## What is uncertain or unverified

**Coverage is the largest gap.** The figures are national population coverage: 2G about 94%, 3G about 90.5%, 4G about 58.8%, 5G about 18.9% and urban only ([Agence Ecofin](https://www.osiris.sn/zimbabwe-les-consommateurs-face-a-la-fin-de-la-2g-et-de-la-3g.html)). Internet Society Pulse gives 52% for 4G and 12% for 5G, so the safe statement is "4G reaches roughly half to 60% of people". Roughly 16 to 17 million SIM connections and about 41.6% of people using the internet add up to many voice-only users ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-zimbabwe)). None of this says anything about quarry, sand-river, mine or farm roads, which are likely worse than the national figure. No official POTRAZ coverage-by-technology table, no tower-outage statistics and no coverage-hole map were found. Phase 0 measurements and a short driver survey are the only way to close this.

**SMS, WhatsApp and USSD feasibility is unproven.** Prices are list prices from international providers; no Zimbabwean aggregator published a price or delivery statistics. Sender-ID registration claims conflict (one provider says none is required, others say about a week). Two-way SMS and local short codes are unverified. Telecel's one-time-code-only restriction may block job alerts to its subscribers. Meta's live Zimbabwe WhatsApp rate and Zimbabwe onboarding rules were not retrieved. USSD fees and timelines are unpublished, and USSD shares a single-platform failure risk with Econet. No source shows zero-rating is available to a Zimbabwean app.

**The 1.4 truck factor is unvalidated.** The app multiplies car travel time by 1.4 to estimate truck time. Since #78 (merged) the customer's own screen and the shared tracking link both use it, so they agree. Nothing in the research tests that figure against real tipper trips. Treat the customer's ETA as a guide: show a range or round it, and calibrate the factor from completed trips once the capture times in Phase 1 exist.

**Nothing was tested on a real phone.** No device or emulator was run. Unobserved: how the WebView error page looks, whether the WebView serves any cached document, whether the camera or Maps hand-off kills the app and the photo with it, whether the browser's online flag is accurate in the shell, whether Web Locks, IndexedDB persistence and the Origin Private File System behave in drivers' WebView versions, and how MapLibre and WebGL run on low-end phones. No maintainer-verified report exists of `server.url` combined with a service worker, and no confirmed way to run a standard PWA plugin with TanStack Start. Whether Play accepts a foreground-service-only design for ConZ is unverified.

**Other gaps.** The code audit read git, not the live database (migrations 0077 to 0108 are described as live but unrecorded, and whether 0126 is live is contradicted between two repo documents). Several numbers are my assumptions to be measured: the 250 to 400 KB photo target, the 24 and 72 hour tiers, the 15 minute "live" window, queue caps, and effort estimates. The OSRM demo server is licensed for non-commercial use and CARTO's commercial terms cap free use, so mapping and routing costs are unknown. Plus Code awareness among Zimbabwean drivers and customers is unevidenced. No Zimbabwe incident data exists for mock-GPS, photo fraud or PIN scams, so the threat ranking is judgement. Legal admissibility of electronic delivery evidence, and POTRAZ data-controller licensing for ConZ, were not researched beyond summaries. Two of the notes disagree on queuing the PIN, which is why it is a decision and not a recommendation. Vendor claims (Capawesome, Transistorsoft, Geotab community figures) are marketing or competitor-sourced.

## Questions only the owner can answer

1. **Late PIN and money.** If a correct PIN is verified hours after the drop, should the job settle automatically, wait for admin review beyond a time limit, or wait for the customer's confirmation? What is the limit (the notes suggest 24 hours normal, 72 hours admin only)?
2. **"Delivered, waiting for confirmation."** Are customers and drivers willing to see this status instead of "Completed" while evidence is queued?
3. **Texts.** Which events, if any, justify a server SMS at US$0.10 to 0.33 each, and who pays: ConZ, the driver, or the customer? Is ConZ willing to request local aggregator quotes and register a sender ID?
4. **Android release appetite.** Will you ship a new Android build and take on a Play foreground-service declaration, a persistent notification and possibly a paid plugin? Who runs Play Console?
5. **Human fallback.** Will a dispatcher phone line be staffed, and for what hours? Who decides "unable to locate", "customer cannot give the PIN" and late-evidence cases, and how quickly?
6. **Customer rules.** Do you want to require a precise pin, or a gate photo, for off-road sites (farms, mines, new plots)? May the customer name a receiver?
7. **No automatic punishment.** Do you confirm that no driver is penalised or banned on a single flag, lateness or clock difference?
8. **Measurement and privacy.** May the app log device, network and GPS-gap data per driver session? Has ConZ's POTRAZ data-controller position been checked, given the route trail, photos and any SMS logs?
9. **Mapping budget.** Will ConZ pay for a licensed router or commercial map tiles, or accept the work to self-host? Is the CARTO key valid in production today?
10. **Drivers' reality.** Where are the dead zones on your busiest routes, which phones do your drivers actually use, and which drivers would pilot Phase 1?
11. **The 1.4 truck factor.** Is it your own figure from real trips, and are you comfortable showing the customer a range instead of a single arrival time?
12. **The stuck jobs.** Do the seven direct-pay jobs stuck 19 to 88 days with a pickup photo and no delivery photo have a known cause?

## Conclusion

The surprising result is how little of this needs a network, a native release or a vendor. The hard stop is a data-only architecture choice: ConZ records evidence at the moment of upload and treats a successful upload as the only proof. Moving the proof to the moment of capture, and the verdict to the moment the server receives it, removes the stop with web changes and two small migrations, and it makes disputes better, because today's upload-time stamps would already misreport any slow upload. The peer evidence also says what not to build: no source shows anyone checking a delivery code offline, and the one idea that needs the driver's phone to hold a secret (local PIN checking) fails on arithmetic.

What the research cannot yet say is how often drivers are actually in dead zones, which decides whether the expensive pieces (offline map packs, background GPS, SMS) earn their cost. That is why Phase 0 is measurement, and why the order of work follows evidence ConZ can collect within weeks rather than assumptions about Zimbabwe's coverage. The honest summary for the owner is that ConZ can promise a driver "you can always finish, and we will confirm when your signal returns", but it cannot yet promise "you will always find the gate", because that depends on the quality of the drop-off description the customer supplies and on a phone call that no software replaces.
